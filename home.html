K-pop (Korean Pop Music) originated in South Korea and became popular in the 1990s, influenced by Western music styles such as hip-hop, R&B, dance, and rock. The modern K-pop industry began with Seo Taiji and Boys, who transformed Korean music and youth culture.

In the late 1990s, entertainment companies introduced the idol system, producing carefully trained boy bands and girl groups. By the 2000s, artists like BoA and TVXQ helped K-pop expand internationally.

The global success of K-pop in the 2010s was driven by social media, with groups like BTS and BLACKPINK leading worldwide popularity. Today, K-pop is known for its catchy music, powerful performances, fashion, and visuals, while also facing criticism for strict management and commercialization. It has become a global cultural phenomenon.
